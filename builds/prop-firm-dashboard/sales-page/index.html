How it actually works

1

You enter your real numbers

Account tier, current balance, high-water mark, days remaining — plus your own journal's average daily P&L and its standard deviation. Not a demo number. Yours.

2

It refuses to run without them

No default win rate standing in for your edge. If you don't have your own avg/std P&L from a real trading journal, the tool won't fabricate one for you — that's the entire honest-hook, not a tagline.

3

It runs a real Monte Carlo simulation

Thousands of simulated trading paths against the tier's actual trailing-drawdown floor (tracked against your high-water mark, not your starting balance) and the days you have left.

4

You get the split, not just a pass rate

Pass probability, plus how much of the failure risk is drawdown-breach vs. simply running out of time — two very different problems with two different fixes.

5

Compare sizes before you trade, not after

Run the same simulation across contract counts side by side, to see exactly where adding size stops helping your pass rate and starts working against it.

THE ONE THING WORTH UNDERSTANDING

Win rate isn't the whole story.

A trader who wins 65% of the time but lets losers run can still blow the trailing drawdown floor. A trader who wins 40% of the time with tight, consistent risk can clear it. What actually decides the eval is expectancy (are your average wins bigger than your average losses, after frequency) crossed with variance (how much your daily results swing) against a floor that moves with your own high-water mark.

That's exactly why this tool doesn't ask for your win rate. It asks for your average and standard deviation of daily P&L — because those two numbers already encode both expectancy and variance, the actual inputs that determine whether you clear the drawdown floor before you run out of days.

Questions

Why does it need my own P&L numbers?

Because a pass probability built on an assumed win rate is a guess wearing a percentage sign. Your actual average and standard deviation of daily P&L are the only inputs that make the simulation mean anything for you specifically.

Does it guarantee I'll pass?

No — nothing honest can. It gives you a probability and a breakdown of why you'd fail (drawdown vs. time), so you can size and pace the eval with real information instead of hope.

Which prop firms does this work for?

Built against TPT's published tier structure (25K–150K, trailing drawdown tracked from the high-water mark). The underlying math generalizes to any evaluation with a trailing-drawdown rule and a time limit — TPT's numbers are simply what's wired in today.

Is this financial advice?

No. It's a sizing and probability calculator run on numbers you provide. It doesn't recommend trades or strategies.

Do I need to give my email to use it?

No. The tool itself has no paywall, no account, and no card required. The email signup above is only for hearing about updates — entirely optional.
