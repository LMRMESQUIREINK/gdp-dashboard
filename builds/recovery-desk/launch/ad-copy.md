# Recovery Desk — Launch Copy (Build Brain Stage 6)

Built via `.claude/skills/launch-pad`. Every variant reuses the sales
page's own hooks and `design-rules.md`'s do-say voice — none of these
invent a new pitch. Per the source opportunity report's own distribution
plan (*"the first 100 customers come from helpful replies, not paid ads
at $4.60 CPC"*), the "medium" format below is deliberately framed as a
**reply inside an existing complaint thread**, not a cold DM — r/ouraring
and r/whoop's own community norms (and basic decency) rule out
unsolicited outreach; replying to someone who already posted the exact
complaint this product answers is the entire strategy.

## Short (Reddit/social reply, 1–3 sentences)

> Same complaint here — Oura's Advisor is basically a nicer-looking
> "we don't know either." Built a text that just says train hard / back
> off / eat more / sleep earlier every morning instead, no dashboard.
> Free 7-day trial if you want to try it on your own data.

> Whoop's coach doing the same thing for you — reading everything,
> saying nothing? Made something that just picks one of four calls
> every morning and tells you why in one line. No app to open. Free
> trial, no card.

## Medium (reply inside an existing complaint thread, ~4–6 sentences)

> Been exactly where you are with this. [Ring/strap]'s built-in coach
> reads like it's trying to sound smart instead of just telling you
> what to do. I built something small on the side: it reads your
> overnight [Oura/Whoop] data and sends one text every morning — train
> hard, back off, eat more, or sleep earlier, plus the one number behind
> the call. No dashboard, no login, nothing to configure. It's a free
> 7-day trial, no card required, and if a reading ever looks like
> something other than normal training fatigue it tells you to check in
> with a doctor instead of guessing. If you want to try it on your own
> data this week, happy to set you up — no pitch, just curious if it's
> more useful than what you've got now.

## Long (email / standalone post — pain → mechanism → offer, same CTA/price as the sales page)

> **Subject: The dashboard was never the product**
>
> If you own an Oura ring or a Whoop strap, you've probably had this
> exact moment: you open the app, the "readiness" score tells you
> something, the AI coach elaborates on it at length, and you still
> don't actually know what to do today.
>
> You're not imagining it. A Whoop member said they're not renewing
> year two because the coach "doesn't even know what day of the week it
> is" for £27/month. An Oura owner said screenshotting their own data
> into ChatGPT gives a better read than the paid Advisor. That's not a
> knock on the hardware — the sensors are good. It's the layer on top
> that's the problem.
>
> Recovery Desk is the fix: it reads your overnight data and sends one
> decision text every morning — train hard, back off, eat more, or
> sleep earlier — plus one line on why, compared against your own
> baseline, not a generic target. No dashboard. No app to open. If a
> reading looks like more than ordinary training fatigue, it tells you
> to see a doctor instead of guessing — that's a hard rule in the code,
> not a disclaimer bolted on after.
>
> $29/mo or $290/yr, free 7-day trial, no card required, cancel any
> time by texting STOP. [Start your free trial →]

## What's out of scope for launch copy

No countdown timers, no invented "X people signed up today" counters,
no fabricated testimonials — this launches with zero customers, and the
copy above doesn't pretend otherwise. Every stat and quote used
(the Whoop/Oura Reddit quotes, the 46%/83% wearable-ownership figures,
Bevel's $1.13M/mo) traces directly to the source opportunity report, not
invented for the ad.
